    CHROMATICA

    Every cell on this page is a real colour, extracted by k-means clustering from the photograph of a real painting. Nothing here is decorative: no colour has been chosen, corrected, or invented. Click any cell to see the work it came from.

    HOW TO READ IT

    It is set as a page of text. Every colour is a letter, every painting is a word, every century is a paragraph — and the reading order is the chronology: earliest at the top left, latest at the bottom right. A word is never broken across a line, which is why the right edge is ragged.

    The year each line opens with is printed in the margin, and the first line of every century is lit. Scroll or drag to read on; the arrow keys step from one painting to the next in date order; the strip at the very bottom is the whole span, and clicking it jumps to a year.

    METHOD

      WHAT THE AXIS MEANS

      Position is time and nothing else. Nothing is sorted by colour, grouped by hue, or ordered by any derived measure: the colours inside a painting stay in the order the extraction produced them, largest pixel share first, and the paintings stay in date order.

      The axis is ordinal rather than linear. A line holds as many paintings as fit, so a line may cover thirty years where the collection is thin and a single year where it is dense — coverage varies about sixtyfold between decades. The margin carries the real year of every line, and the strip at the bottom reports works per year on a linear axis, so the unevenness stays visible instead of being flattened by the layout. This is a survey of one museum's public-domain holdings, not of art history.

      The page stops around 1910, and it stops rather than looping. That is a copyright boundary, not an aesthetic one: paintings after it are almost never in the public domain, so the twentieth century cannot be shown honestly here.
