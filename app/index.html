    CHROMATICA

    Every cell on this tape is a real colour, extracted by k-means clustering from the photograph of a real painting. Nothing here is decorative: no colour has been chosen, corrected, or invented. Click any cell to see the work it came from.

    HOW TO READ IT

    The tape is wound on a drum, so only about a fifth of it is on screen at once — drag or scroll to wind it, and the strip at the very bottom shows where you are on the whole span. Cells run 14 pixels at the crown and compress to 6 at the rim; the curvature is what hides the ends, and no colour is altered by it.

    METHOD

      WHAT THE AXIS MEANS

      Horizontal position is time and nothing else. The axis is ordinal rather than linear: each column of the tape holds roughly the same number of paintings, so one column may span a century where the collection is thin and two years where it is dense. The ticks under the tape carry the real year, and the strip at the bottom reports works per year — so the unevenness stays visible instead of being flattened by the layout. This is a survey of one museum's public-domain holdings, not of art history.

      The tape stops around 1910, and it stops rather than looping. That is a copyright boundary, not an aesthetic one: paintings after it are almost never in the public domain, so the twentieth century cannot be shown honestly here.
